Landscape art brief

Homebound is a free iPhone app for people heading outside. You plan a trip, choose who should know, and they hear from Homebound if you aren't back on time.

I'm looking for an artist to create the mountain landscape at the top of the app's Home screen. Home is the first screen people see every time they open the app. This page has everything you need to make it.

One landscape, 30 versions

Create one landscape. The mountains, the trees, and the layout stay the same in every version; only the light and the weather change. Moving from one version to the next should feel like the same place at a different hour.

When the time of day changes, the app fades from one image to the next over about a second. A change in the weather swaps the image straight away.

Five times of day

The app works these out from the real sunrise and sunset where the phone is. Without the phone's location it uses fixed hours: dawn 6 to 9 am, day 9 am to 5 pm, golden hour 5 to 7 pm, twilight 7 to 8 pm, night the rest.

The overdue sky

When someone's trip is overdue or they press SOS, Home calms the sky down so the red warning card stands out. The app does this itself by washing your clear scene out toward the page color, with no sun, moon, stars or weather. You don't need to create anything for it, but at those times your art will show very faded. Pick Overdue (calm) above to see how much.

What you create, what the app adds

My plan is that you create the scenery and the app keeps drawing a few small things on top.

You createThe app draws on top
The sky's color and light for each time of day.
The mountains, trees and ground.
Clouds, fog, the rainy and stormy skies, and snow on the peaks.
The lightning bolt, on its own layer. 		The sun and the moon. The moon shows its real phase each night, from a thin crescent to full.
The stars. A few of them twinkle slowly.
Falling snow and rain, slow and light.

Why: the moon's phase and the motion only work if the app draws them live. It also keeps the image files smaller. All the motion stops when someone turns on Reduce Motion or Low Power Mode on their phone.

So please leave the sun, moon, stars and falling snow and rain out of your images, but light the scene as if they're there. Today the sun and moon sit 132 points (396 px) above the bottom of the sky, at these spots across the width: dawn 16%, day 56%, golden hour 80%, twilight 76%, night 84%. I can move them to suit your composition. Mark where you'd like them.

I'd like your opinion

Is this the right split? Here are the other ways I've thought about. Tell me which you'd pick and why.

  • You design the sun and moon into each scene. They'd match your style better, but the moon would look the same every night. The app would still add the stars, snow and rain.
  • You design the sun, moon, stars, snowflakes and raindrops as separate pieces in your style. The app places and moves them, and shapes your moon to the real phase. Everything matches and the motion still works, but it's more to make.
  • You create everything as still images, falling snow and rain included. Nothing moves and the app only swaps pictures.

If your style needs something I haven't thought of, say so.

Size and layout

Home is shown on iPhones held upright, from 375 to 440 points wide. iPhones use 3 pixels per point, so design at 3×. Your canvas is 1320 × 1500 px, which is 440 × 500 points.

The bottom of your canvas is the bottom of the sky. The app shows the image at full size, lined up on that bottom edge. On narrower phones the sides are cut off evenly, so keep anything that matters within the middle 1125 px (375 points). At the default text size, the bottom 990 px (330 points) of your canvas is on screen.

Keeping the greeting readable

This is the one firm rule. The greeting and the line under it have to be easy to read on every version, for everyone, including people with low vision. That means a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 between the text and the sky right behind it, in the zone from 480 to 990 px.

  • Dawn, day and golden hour, in every weather except storm, use dark text: #1E3323 for the greeting and #4B5348 for the line under it. The sky behind them must be at least as light as a #C3C3C3 gray. Lighter is safer.
  • Twilight, night and every storm use light text, #F6F1E6. The sky behind it must be at least as dark as a #6E6E6E gray. Darker is safer.
  • Keep texture, grain and detail low in that zone, so the letters don't fight the background.

If a sky you love fails, tell us. We can sometimes adjust the text color instead.

Style

Textured flat, like a national park poster. Layered flat shapes, soft gradients in the sky, a fine grain and a little hand-made texture, and a limited palette. Depth comes from ridges that get lighter and bluer with distance. It should be richer than what's there today, but still simple enough to read at a glance behind text.

Generic Mountain West. Rocky peaks, conifers, perhaps some aspens and a valley floor. It should feel like the Rockies or the Wasatch without being anyone's exact skyline.

Keep it quiet. No text or logos, and nothing that pulls the eye away from the greeting. People see this every time they open the app, so it should be lovely on the hundredth look, not only the first.

Sit well with the brand. The landscape should feel at home next to Homebound's colors, especially the forest green card that overlaps its bottom edge. The full palette is on the brand colors page.

The files

  • 30 Home scenes as PNG, 1320 × 1500 px, sRGB, no transparency. Name them sky-<time>-<weather>.png, as in the grid below.
  • The lightning layer as one transparent PNG, 1320 × 1500 px, with the bolt where it should sit, named sky-lightning.png. If it needs to look different at night, send one for each time of day (sky-lightning-night.png and so on).
  • Your layered source file (Photoshop, Procreate, Illustrator, Affinity or similar), with the sky, each ridge, the trees, clouds, fog, snow and texture on separate layers.
  • Texture on its own layer. Grain makes image files much bigger, and the app has to carry all 30. Please send each scene with and without the grain, plus the grain on its own as a seamless transparent tile (for example 512 × 512 px). Then the app can add it once over the top if that keeps the app smaller.
  • Where the sun and moon go for each time of day, as a marked layer in the source file or a short note.
