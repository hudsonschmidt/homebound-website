Getting Started

A personal safety app: create trip plans with an expected return time, add safety contacts, and if you don't check out on time your contacts are automatically notified with your trip details and location.

Passwordless sign-in: Continue with Email for a one-time code, or Continue with Apple. Your account is created the first time you sign in. Your safety contacts never need an account.

Anything where someone should know where you're going and when you'll be back: hikes, walks home at night, kids' field trips, nights out, even grocery runs.

Usually a few shorter ones. A trip has a single expected return time, so one that covers a whole day only really checks on you once, at the very end. Splitting the day into legs — drive out, hike, drive home — gives your contacts a real signal at each step and lets you keep each grace period tight.

It also helps your battery. Anything a trip is running, like live location, ends when that leg ends instead of running all day.

Start from the Home tab. You get three ways to go:

  • A Quick Trip — tap one of your saved presets and you're off in a single tap.
  • Plan a trip (Solo or Group) — the full planner: activity, location, start time and ETA, grace period, notes, and safety contacts.
  • One-time trip — a fast setup for something you won't do again, without saving a preset.

Free accounts can assign 2 safety contacts per trip; Homebound+ allows 5.

Check-ins & Reminders

A check-in is an optional progress update. Checking out ends your trip and tells everyone you're safe. That's the one that matters: miss it and your contacts are alerted.

Optional nudges to check in at an interval you choose. They're just for you; contacts never see them. Free.

No. Missed check-ins alert no one; contacts are only notified if you miss your checkout (ETA plus grace period). This is the part people most often get backwards.

Yes. When you plan a trip, turn on Quiet hours and choose when check-in reminders stop and start again, for example no reminders from 10 PM until 8 AM. To use quiet hours on every new trip, go to Settings > Customization and turn on Quiet Hours under Trip Defaults. Emergency alerts always come through.

If your trip has a destination, Homebound notices when you get within about 150 metres of it and sends you an "Arrived at…?" notification suggesting you check out. It never checks you out on its own — "Not yet" dismisses it and the trip carries on.

It comes back each time you leave that radius and return, so a destination set at or near where you're actually spending the trip can prompt you repeatedly. Either set the destination to where the trip really ends, or start the trip from your current location without one. Checking out stops the prompts for good.

Arrival reminders need location access. With "Always" they arrive whenever you get there; with "While Using" they only fire while Homebound is open.

To say more, tap Reply and pick a quick reply or write your own, up to 200 characters. Any check-in, extension, check-out, or SOS also answers an open request, so you don't need to do both.

Requests, messages, and your replies all appear in the trip timeline in Trip Details. The Trip details button on your trip card shows a count when something is waiting.

Live Location Sharing

If you go overdue, they also get your last known location in the overdue email, and on your own trip that page shows your live position. It's optional and is turned on per trip.

Live location has a main switch and a per-trip choice. First turn on Allow Live Location in Settings > Privacy. Then, when you plan a trip with a friend as a safety contact, turn on Live location for friends. For a Quick Trip, the option is called Share Live Location.

Sharing only starts once the trip is active. It won't run for a trip that's still scheduled for later. If the main switch is off, the trip planner shows "Enable in Settings > Privacy to share location" with an Enable button.

Yes, more than anything else in Homebound. While a trip with live sharing is running, Homebound keeps GPS at full accuracy so your position on your friends' map stays accurate.

It sends your position about once a minute. It sends more often while someone has your trip open on their map, once you're past your ETA, and after an SOS. In Low Power Mode it sends less often when no one is watching. Sharing stops the moment the trip ends or you turn it off. For a long day, start with a full battery or bring a charger. If nobody needs to watch a live map, leaving live location off is the biggest thing you can do for battery.

Yes. There's no switch on the trip screen itself, but Settings > Privacy > "Allow Live Location" turns it off everywhere, including a trip that's already running. Your phone stops sending positions right away, and our servers stop storing them for you.

The trip carries on exactly as before: your ETA, check-ins, overdue alerts, and SOS all work the same, and your contacts still get everything they normally would. Turn the switch back on and sharing resumes the next time the app refreshes your active trip.

If your trip has a destination, Homebound also watches for you arriving so it can remind you to check out. That uses the same low-power geofencing iOS uses for reminders, not continuous GPS.

  • Leave live location off unless someone genuinely needs the live map. Your check-ins still carry your position when you send them.
  • Split a long day into a few shorter trips instead of one that runs from morning to night.

  • Check out when you're done. Everything the trip was running stops there.

Trip live location is tied to a specific trip and your safety contacts. A Circle is separate from trips entirely — it's ongoing location sharing with friends you pick, on your schedule, whether or not you're on a trip. Both are also different from contact groups, which are simply a way to add several saved safety contacts to a trip at once.

Open the Map tab and tap the people icon at the top left. That's where Circles live. Create a Circle, name it, and add friends. Then pick how long to share for (1 hour, 4 hours, 8 hours, 1 day, 3 days, or 1 week) and tap Share My Location. Discover

Discover suggests interesting places near you based on your current location and the weather. It needs location access and an internet connection, and it's free. Discover doesn't create a trip for you — it's just for finding somewhere to go.

For places — trailheads, crags, campgrounds, breweries — tapping opens directions in Apple Maps. Concerts work differently: the card shows the date, start time, price, and venue, and tapping takes you to the event page to get tickets rather than to directions.

Discover covers ten activities: hiking, running, climbing, biking, camping, surfing, skiing, snowboarding, bars & breweries, and nearby concerts. Some are seasonal — for example, ski and snowboard spots show in winter and surfing in the warmer months — so what you see changes with the time of year and conditions.

If Homebound searched for an activity and couldn't find good matches near you, it lists that activity under Nothing Nearby rather than show low-quality or irrelevant results. That usually means there aren't strong options close by right now. If the whole tab is empty, make sure location access is enabled.

Discover checks the weather and the season before suggesting places. Activities that don't suit current conditions are grouped by the reason: Too Hot, Too Cold, Rain Likely, Too Windy, Out of Season, or Conditions Unavailable. Open a group and tap Show anyway next to an activity to search for spots regardless. Check the conditions yourself before you go.

Yes. You'll see current conditions for your destination when creating a trip and on completed trip details. You can also save Favorite Locations in the Weather view to check conditions and get alerts when the weather looks great for the activities you choose.

Safety Insights & Score

Your safety score is a 0-100 rating computed from your trip history. It's based on three factors:

  • On-time rate (40%): Did you check out before your ETA plus grace period expired?
  • Check-in rate (30%): Did you check in during trips where you enabled check-in reminders?
  • Contact coverage (30%): How many safety contacts did you assign to your trips? 0 contacts scores 0%, 1 scores 33%, 2 scores 67%, and 3 or more scores 100%. Email contacts and friends both count.

Scores are graded as: A+ (90-100), A (80-89), B (70-79), C (60-69), D (50-59), F (below 50).

Only completed trips count. Cancelled trips are left out of the calculation entirely, and trips still in progress don't count until they're finished.

Check-in rate only applies to trips where you enabled check-in reminders (set a check-in interval). If you don't use check-in reminders, this factor doesn't affect your score negatively.

When you do enable check-in reminders:

  • Checking in at least once during your trip gives you full credit for that trip
  • Skipping all check-ins on a trip where you enabled reminders counts as 0% for that trip
  • Your overall check-in rate is the average across all trips with reminders enabled

Trip length tiers: To keep scoring fair, trip length is capped based on duration:

  • Under 1 hour: capped at 60 minutes
  • 1-4 hours: capped at 120 minutes
  • 4-8 hours: capped at 240 minutes
  • 8+ hours: capped at 480 minutes

Formula: rate = (check-ins ÷ capped length) × 1200, maxing out at 100%.

Examples:

  • 6-hour hike (240 min cap), 3 check-ins: (3 ÷ 240) × 1200 = 15 → caps at 100%
  • 2-hour trip (120 min cap), 1 check-in: (1 ÷ 120) × 1200 = 10 → caps at 100%
  • 2-hour trip (120 min cap), 0 check-ins: (0 ÷ 120) × 1200 = 0%

The weekly recap summarizes your adventure activity for the current week. It shows your trip count, total adventure hours, whether all trips were completed safely, any overdue incidents, and your busiest day of the week.

No. Your safety score and weekly recap are computed entirely on your device using trip data already stored locally. No additional data is collected, transmitted, or stored on our servers for these features.

Three things move it: check out before your ETA plus grace period expires; check in at least once on trips where you've set a check-in interval; and add more safety contacts, since three or more earns full coverage credit. Cancelling a trip doesn't hurt your score — cancelled trips are excluded — but it doesn't help it either. Consistent habits raise the score over time.

Group Trips

Group trips let you create a single trip with multiple participants, such as group hikes, ski trips, or dive excursions. Everyone can check in and add photos to the same trip album. Members can use Find to locate each other or set a meetup spot, and can see each other on the map if the trip's location settings allow it.

When you create the trip you choose who can end it:

  • Anyone (the default) — any participant can end the trip for everyone.
  • Vote — a share of participants has to agree first. You set the threshold anywhere from 50% to 100%.
  • Only Me — only you, the trip leader, can end it.

You can also switch on Let everyone finish individually on the Group Finish Rules screen: each person taps "I'm home" as they arrive, everyone gets told, and the trip ends on its own once the last person is back.

A group trip supports up to 20 participants, including the trip owner.

When creating a trip, add participants by searching for friends who use Homebound. They'll receive an invitation notification and can accept to join your trip.

Yes. The trip owner — or any participant, if the owner allows it — can tap "Add" on an active group trip to invite more friends.

Yes. If you're a participant (not the owner), you can leave an active group trip from the trip menu. The trip continues for everyone else.

Yes. Group trips are available to all users regardless of subscription status.

Reporting & Blocking

On someone else's feed post, open the "…" menu and choose Report Post. On a comment, use the comment's menu and choose Report Comment. For a photo in a trip album, tap the "…" button on the photo and choose Report Photo. For a message on your trip, use the "…" menu on it in the trip timeline and choose Report Message. If you're someone's safety contact, you can also report a message they send you from the Check On sheet. To report a person, open their profile from the Friends tab and choose Report. You pick a reason, such as spam, harassment or bullying, inappropriate content, or impersonation, and can add details. We aim to review every report within 24 hours, and reports about safety, minors, or threats come first.

Open their profile from the Friends tab and choose Block, or use the "…" menu on their post or comment. Blocking ends the friendship, hides their content from you and yours from them (including each other's trips in the feed), and stops them from adding you as a friend again. It also stops messages, check-in requests, Find, and meetup pins between you. You each stop being the other's safety contact on every trip, including one running now, and you leave each other's Circles. Contacts you added by email aren't affected.

Settings > Privacy > Blocked Users lists everyone you've blocked and lets you unblock them. Unblocking doesn't restore the friendship — if you want to be friends again, one of you has to send a new invite.
