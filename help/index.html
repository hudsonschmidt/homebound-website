Getting Started

A personal safety app: create trip plans with an expected return time, add safety contacts, and if you don't check out on time your contacts are automatically notified with your trip details and location.

Check-ins & Reminders

A check-in is an optional progress update. Checking out ends your trip and tells everyone you're safe. That's the one that matters: miss it and your contacts are alerted.

Optional nudges to check in at an interval you choose. They're just for you; contacts never see them. Free.

No. Missed check-ins alert no one; contacts are only notified if you miss your checkout (ETA plus grace period). This is the part people most often get backwards.

Yes, per trip: turn on "notification hours" (default 8 AM–10 PM). Emergency alerts always come through.

If your trip has a destination, Homebound notices when you get within about 150 metres of it and sends you an "Arrived at…?" notification suggesting you check out. It never checks you out on its own — "Not yet" dismisses it and the trip carries on.

It comes back each time you leave that radius and return, so a destination set at or near where you're actually spending the trip can prompt you repeatedly. Either set the destination to where the trip really ends, or start the trip from your current location without one. Checking out stops the prompts for good.

Arrival reminders need location access. With "Always" they arrive whenever you get there; with "While Using" they only fire while Homebound is open.

Apple Watch

The watch app shows your active trip and its countdown, and puts the actions that matter on your wrist:

  • Check in
  • Extend your ETA (30 minutes up to 4 hours)
  • Finish the trip — or tap "I'm home" on a group trip that uses individual finish
  • Send an SOS

It's a companion to the iPhone app rather than a standalone one: you sign in and create trips on your phone, and the watch handles what happens once a trip is running. An SOS sent from the watch goes out without a typed message.

Not for the actions above. Your phone syncs the trip and a signing token to the watch, so as long as the watch itself has a connection — cellular or Wi-Fi — check in, extend, finish, and SOS go straight to Homebound with the phone nowhere nearby. You do need your iPhone to sign in the first time, to create trips, and to keep the watch's trip data current. If the watch has nothing synced, it'll ask you to open Homebound on your phone.

Yes. Homebound offers complications in the circular, rectangular, inline, and corner styles, showing your trip status and a live countdown. Adding one also helps the watch refresh in the background more often. If the watch hasn't heard from your phone in roughly half a day, the complication switches to a quiet "sync" state instead of showing a stale countdown — a safety complication shouldn't imply it's current when it isn't.

Yes, and it works with no signal at all. When a trip syncs, the watch schedules its own haptic alerts for your ETA and for the end of your grace period, so they fire even deep in the backcountry. Checking in, extending, or finishing moves or clears them. The alerts include a "Check In" button so you can respond straight from the notification. They need notification permission for Homebound on the watch — if that's off, the app says so on the trip screen rather than letting a safety alert fail quietly.

If your phone is streaming live location on a trip and then goes out of range, the watch offers — once per trip — to carry the stream, sending a position on its background refresh cadence (roughly every 15 minutes, though watchOS decides the actual timing; a Homebound complication on your face helps). The phone stays in charge whenever it's reachable. Watch updates are less frequent than the phone's, and the trip screen shows when the last position actually landed rather than promising a schedule.

Live Location Sharing

Live location sharing lets your friends and group-trip participants see your real-time position during an active trip, updated continuously on a map in their app. (Email contacts instead see your most recent check-in location through the "Follow This Trip" link.) It's completely optional and is enabled per trip.

When creating a trip, toggle "Share Live Location" to enable it for that specific trip. You can also set a default under Settings > Privacy > "Allow Live Location." Live location only activates once you start the trip (when it becomes active) — it won't run for a trip that's still scheduled for later.

Yes, noticeably. While a trip with live sharing is running, Homebound holds the GPS at full accuracy and sends your position as often as every 30 seconds. That's what makes the dot on your friends' map move in real time, and it's the most power-hungry thing the app does.

It stops the moment the trip ends or you turn sharing off. For a long day, start with a full battery or bring a charger. If nobody actually needs to watch a live map, leaving live location off is the single biggest thing you can do for battery.

Yes. There's no switch on the trip screen itself, but Settings > Privacy > "Allow Live Location" turns it off everywhere, including a trip that's already running. Your phone stops sending positions right away, and our servers stop storing them for you.

The trip carries on exactly as before: your ETA, check-ins, overdue alerts, and SOS all work the same, and your contacts still get everything they normally would. Turn the switch back on and sharing resumes the next time the app refreshes your active trip.

Circle sharing is separate. It isn't affected by this switch and has its own Stop button.

It asks for a single position at the moments that need one, then stops: when you check in, when you extend your ETA, and when you send an SOS. Nothing runs in between.

If your trip has a destination, Homebound also watches for you arriving so it can remind you to check out. That uses the same low-power geofencing iOS uses for reminders, not continuous GPS.

  • Leave live location off unless someone genuinely needs the live map. Your check-ins still carry your position when you send them.
  • Split a long day into a few shorter trips instead of one that runs from morning to night.
  • Set the ETA you actually expect. A trip that outlives your day keeps running with it.
  • Check out when you're done. Everything the trip was running stops there.

Accuracy depends on your device's GPS capabilities and environmental conditions. In open areas with clear sky view, accuracy is typically within a few meters. In canyons, dense forests, or indoors, accuracy may be reduced. Live location should be treated as an approximate guide, not a precise tracking system.

Circles

A Circle is a named group of friends you choose to share your live location with — for example "Family" or "Roommates." You decide exactly who's in each Circle and when to share. Circles are free; you can create up to 20, with up to 50 people each.

Trip live location is tied to a specific trip and your safety contacts. A Circle is separate from trips entirely — it's ongoing location sharing with friends you pick, on your schedule, whether or not you're on a trip. Both are also different from contact groups, which are simply a way to add several saved safety contacts to a trip at once.

Open the Map tab and tap the people icon at the top right — that's where Circles lives. Create a Circle, name it, and add friends. Then pick how long to share for — 1 hour, 4 hours, 8 hours, 1 day, 3 days, or 1 week — and tap "Share My Location." While you're sharing, a bar with a "Stop" button sits above the tab bar wherever you are in the app, so you can end it at any time. You can also stop from inside the Circle itself.

Discover

Discover suggests interesting places near you based on your current location and the weather. It needs location access and an internet connection, and it's free. Discover doesn't create a trip for you — it's just for finding somewhere to go.

For places — trailheads, crags, campgrounds, breweries — tapping opens directions in Apple Maps. Concerts work differently: the card shows the date, start time, price, and venue, and tapping takes you to the event page to get tickets rather than to directions.

Discover covers ten activities: hiking, running, climbing, biking, camping, surfing, skiing, snowboarding, bars & breweries, and nearby concerts. Some are seasonal — for example, ski and snowboard spots show in winter and surfing in the warmer months — so what you see changes with the time of year and conditions.

If Homebound can't find good matches for an activity near you, it says "Nothing nearby" rather than show low-quality or irrelevant results. That usually just means there aren't strong options close by right now, or the activity is out of season. If the whole tab is empty, make sure location access is enabled.

Yes. You'll see current conditions for your destination when creating a trip and on completed trip details. You can also save Favorite Locations in the Weather view to check conditions and get alerts when the weather looks great for the activities you choose.

Safety Insights & Score

Your safety score is a 0-100 rating computed from your trip history. It's based on three factors:

  • On-time rate (40%): Did you check out before your ETA plus grace period expired?
  • Check-in rate (30%): Did you check in during trips where you enabled check-in reminders?
  • Contact coverage (30%): How many safety contacts did you assign to your trips? 0 contacts scores 0%, 1 scores 33%, 2 scores 67%, and 3 or more scores 100%. Email contacts and friends both count.

Scores are graded as: A+ (90-100), A (80-89), B (70-79), C (60-69), D (50-59), F (below 50).

Only completed trips count. Cancelled trips are left out of the calculation entirely, and trips still in progress don't count until they're finished.

Check-in rate only applies to trips where you enabled check-in reminders (set a check-in interval). If you don't use check-in reminders, this factor doesn't affect your score negatively.

When you do enable check-in reminders:

  • Checking in at least once during your trip gives you full credit for that trip
  • Skipping all check-ins on a trip where you enabled reminders counts as 0% for that trip
  • Your overall check-in rate is the average across all trips with reminders enabled

Trip length tiers: To keep scoring fair, trip length is capped based on duration:

  • Under 1 hour: capped at 60 minutes
  • 1-4 hours: capped at 120 minutes
  • 4-8 hours: capped at 240 minutes
  • 8+ hours: capped at 480 minutes

Formula: rate = (check-ins ÷ capped length) × 1200, maxing out at 100%.

Examples:

  • 6-hour hike (240 min cap), 3 check-ins: (3 ÷ 240) × 1200 = 15 → caps at 100%
  • 2-hour trip (120 min cap), 1 check-in: (1 ÷ 120) × 1200 = 10 → caps at 100%
  • 2-hour trip (120 min cap), 0 check-ins: (0 ÷ 120) × 1200 = 0%

The weekly recap summarizes your adventure activity for the current week. It shows your trip count, total adventure hours, whether all trips were completed safely, any overdue incidents, and your busiest day of the week.

No. Your safety score and weekly recap are computed entirely on your device using trip data already stored locally. No additional data is collected, transmitted, or stored on our servers for these features.

Three things move it: check out before your ETA plus grace period expires; check in at least once on trips where you've set a check-in interval; and add more safety contacts, since three or more earns full coverage credit. Cancelling a trip doesn't hurt your score — cancelled trips are excluded — but it doesn't help it either. Consistent habits raise the score over time.

Group Trips

Group trips let you create a single trip with multiple participants — group hikes, ski trips, dive excursions. Everyone can check in, and each participant's location can be shared with the group.

When you create the trip you choose who can end it:

  • Anyone (the default) — any participant can end the trip for everyone.
  • Vote — a share of participants has to agree first. You set the threshold anywhere from 50% to 100%.
  • Only Me — only you, the trip leader, can end it.

You can also switch on individual finish: each person taps "I'm home" as they arrive, everyone gets told, and the trip ends on its own once the last person is back.

A group trip supports up to 20 participants, including the trip owner.

When creating a trip, add participants by searching for friends who use Homebound. They'll receive an invitation notification and can accept to join your trip.

Yes. The trip owner — or any participant, if the owner allows it — can tap "Add" on an active group trip to invite more friends.

Yes. If you're a participant (not the owner), you can leave an active group trip from the trip menu. The trip continues for everyone else.

If you're a safety contact for several people on the same group trip, Homebound combines them into one "Watching N people" card in your Friends tab, so you can follow everyone at once instead of seeing separate cards.

Yes. Group trips are available to all users regardless of subscription status.

Give 7, Get 7

Invite someone new to Homebound with your invite link. When they join through it and finish their first trip, you each get 7 days of Homebound+. Your dashboard is in Settings under "Give 7, Get 7."

Three things:

  • Your friend has to be new — their account must be created within 72 hours of accepting your invite.
  • They have to complete their first qualifying trip: their own trip finishing, or marking themselves home as a participant on a group trip.
  • That trip must have been active for at least 30 minutes.

Each person can qualify once. Until they finish that trip, they show as "pending first trip" on your dashboard.

You can earn up to 90 days of Homebound+ per rolling 365 days from referrals. Days you earn are banked rather than burned: they only start counting down when you don't already have paid coverage, so subscribing while you have credit doesn't waste it — the unused whole days go back into the bank. Your dashboard shows your balance and how much of the yearly limit you've used.

Settings > Give 7, Get 7 has your link plus a copy button, your referral list, and your balance. It's the same reusable friend invite link and QR code you'd use to add someone as a friend — anyone who joins through it and qualifies counts.

Reporting & Blocking

On someone else's feed post, open the "…" menu and choose Report Post. On a comment, use the comment's menu and choose Report Comment. To report a person, open their profile from the Friends tab and choose Report. You pick a reason — spam, harassment or bullying, inappropriate content, impersonation, or something else — and can add details. Reports are reviewed within 24 hours.

Open their profile from the Friends tab and choose Block. Blocking ends the friendship, hides their content from you and yours from them, and stops them from adding you as a friend again.

Settings > Privacy > Blocked Users lists everyone you've blocked and lets you unblock them. Unblocking doesn't restore the friendship — if you want to be friends again, one of you has to send a new invite.
