Brand Colors

Homebound color palette, use for any Homebound material. Click any hex value to copy it.

Last updated: September 1, 2026

Core palette

These are the five most used and most prominant colors.

Forest Green 53, 107, 61

Primary brand color. Buttons, links, app tint, the main green in the logo. This is “the Homebound green”.

Moss 74, 124, 82

Always paired with Forest Green as the lighter end of a gradient. E.g. avatar rings, headers, call-to-action buttons, etc. Rarely used on its own.

Cream 246, 241, 230

The default background. Homebound uses cream, not white.

Ink 38, 34, 26

Body text. A warm near-black, never pure black.

Copper 184, 115, 51

Warm accent. Comes from the cabin in the logo, and “home” markers on maps. It’s a highlight, not a second brand color.

Extended palette

Supporting colors for backgrounds, states, and secondary text.

Pine 36, 73, 42

Dark section backgrounds. The deep green band behind testimonials on the website.

Deep Forest 30, 51, 35

The website footer. The darkest green in the system.

Paper 253, 251, 245

Cards and panels sitting on top of Cream. A half-step lighter, still not white.

Copper Dark 156, 95, 39

Hover and pressed states for Copper elements.

Amber 233, 168, 58

Warm highlight used for small things. E.g. the sun in the website hero, announcement buttons, etc.

Muted Text 92, 85, 74

Secondary and supporting copy.

Faint Text 138, 130, 116

Captions, timestamps, fine print.

The two golds

Gold changes depending on what’s behind it

✦✦✦✦✦ On light backgrounds Antique Gold #B07D18 · rgb(176, 125, 24)
✦✦✦✦✦ On dark backgrounds Warm Gold #E1B64C · rgb(225, 182, 76)

Antique Gold on a dark background sinks into mud. Warm Gold on cream is too pale to read. They are the same brand color at two weights, not two different colors.

Don’t color-pick the logo to get brand colors. The artwork values are tuned for flat illustration; the palette above is tuned for text and interfaces, which have legibility requirements the logo doesn’t. Use this table only when working on the logo itself (recreating it, placing it on an unusual background, matching a print proof, etc).

Usage guidance

  • Backgrounds. Cream first, Paper for cards on top of it, Pine or Deep Forest when you want a dark section. White is not a brand background.
  • Green on green. Forest Green on Cream is comfortable and legible. Forest Green on Pine is not. On dark green backgrounds, use Cream for text.
